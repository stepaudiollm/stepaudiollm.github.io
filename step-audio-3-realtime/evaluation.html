Scoring and adaptation

Keep the task intact.
Make the output comparable.

The benchmark answer keys and rubrics remain the basis for scoring. Adaptations are limited to the audio-to-text interface, multi-turn context, and output format needed by the public Chat Completions endpoint.

Text-only LLM judges

Open-ended responses are evaluated with a text-only judge. The judge receives the task text, answer key or rubric, and the model's final text response; it does not receive the audio. The general text-based tasks use gpt-oss-120b, while Audio Multi-Challenge uses o4-mini. Where supported, judge requests use temperature 0.8, top_p 0.9, frequency penalty 0.1, and presence penalty 0.1. Empty, failed, or unparsable judge responses are retried before aggregation.

Metric aggregation

Each benchmark keeps its native primary metric. For rubric tasks, rubric items are averaged within a case before cases are averaged with equal weight. If a cross-benchmark summary is needed, metrics are first expressed on a 0–100 scale and then macro-averaged without sample-count weighting. Multiple-choice tasks report accuracy, Audio Multi-Challenge reports APR and ARS, WildSpeech-Bench maps its 1–10 judge score to 0–100, and MTalk-Bench reports a case-level macro pass rate.

Dataset-specific method

Where the request
meets the benchmark.

The following adaptations make the public audio-to-text interface usable while keeping the benchmark's task evidence and scoring structure visible.

Benchmark family Request construction Scoring treatment
MMSU / MMAR / MMAU mini Official question and options are sent with an output-format instruction and audio input. Answer correctness is judged against the official answer structure.
Big Bench Audio The question is contained in the recording; audio is followed by the fixed answer instruction. Open-ended text answers are evaluated against the task answer.
Audio Multi-Challenge Multi-turn audio and text history are preserved for the final response. Official rubric structure with APR / ARS and the o4-mini judge.
WildSpeech-Bench The model receives audio and returns text directly instead of synthesizing a voice response. The checklist-based 1–10 score is mapped linearly to 0–100.
Step-Caption Audio is followed by a natural-language description instruction. The structured description dimensions are assessed by the text judge.
MTalk-Bench Current-turn audio is combined with official text history and previous model replies. Semantic, paralinguistic, and ambient subsets use the same S2T text-rubric process.
